# More Than Gold

A photo-led EDUH4058 homepage by Blair Franciscus, following the day in order: meeting Australian Olympians, walking around Circular Quay, then completing class challenges at the Sydney Opera House and reflecting on national sporting pride.

Open `index.html` in a browser, or run `python3 -m http.server 8000` in this folder and visit http://localhost:8000. No build or dependencies are required. Google Fonts has local fallbacks.

## Portfolio requirements

- Artefact: Australian Olympic kangaroo photograph, `#artefact`.
- Proof of attendance: the portfolio's designated selfie beside the memorial, `#attendance`.
- Statue / monument: the photographed flag memorial and contextual caption, `#statue`.
- Task: five preset class challenges, original scorecard and exact transcribed results, `#doing`.
- Question: a personal comparison of Australian and American Olympic pride, `#reflecting`.

The Opera House flag photograph introduces the page. The photograph with Holly anchors the encounter; the marathon hats supply the surrounding festival context. Original photographs were copied unchanged from user-supplied workspace screenshots.

## Interactions

Native expandable challenge rows show recorded attempts and results. A session-only exploration counter celebrates viewing all five; it does not imply that the visitor completed the sporting tasks. The participation cards and research context also expand. Photos open in a modal with captions, Escape dismissal and focus restoration. A reading-progress bar and active chapter navigation follow scrolling. Entry transitions and confetti respect reduced-motion preferences. Content and native disclosures remain available without JavaScript; photo links then open the original files.

## Editorial basis

The user's supplied account, PDF and scorecard transcription guide the first-person prose. These are reflections, not invented Olympian quotations. Challenges were preset by the class, not independently selected. The swimming result stays “20” without an inferred unit; the printed 2024 competition deadline is identified as worksheet-template text. The “statue” photograph is described as a historical flag memorial, not an Olympic statue.

Official sources linked alongside the text support event details, Olympic histories, the site's historical context and the cultural discussion. Funding is presented as specific institutional evidence rather than an unsupported blanket statement about all athletes. First-person comparisons remain personal observations rather than national generalisations.
